Approval-first AI operations

Other AI tools wait for instructions.
Cognivex hands you finished work.

It reads your inbox, drafts the replies, the invoices and the posts, then puts each one in front of you with the reasoning attached. Nothing is sent, posted, paid or replied to without your approval.

    Tuesday, 9:40am

    Forty-one unread emails, two of which matter. An invoice from March that may or may not have been chased. A post that should have gone up on Friday. A quote someone is still waiting on.

    None of it is hard. All of it is yours — and it is four hours before you get to the work people actually pay you for.

    Forty-one things arrived before you opened the laptop.

    Thirty-eight of them were never going to need you.

    Three were.

    Nothing leaves without you

    Three steps. You are the third one.

    1. It reads

      Your inbox, your order messages, and where every job and payment currently stands.

    2. It drafts

      The reply, the invoice, the follow-up, the post — written out in full, with the reason it wrote it.

    3. You approve

      You read it, edit it, or bin it. Only then does anything leave your business.

    Four things, done properly.

    That is the whole list. Anything not on it is not built.

    • Inbox triage Forty emails in, six decisions out — each with a reply already written. ↗
    • Invoices and follow-up Knows which client has not paid since March, and drafts the chase. ↗
    • Social posts Say what you want posted. It writes it and lays it out; you look before it goes. ↗
    • Memory and priorities What is due, what is overdue, what to do first — and why that order. ↗

    Approval is not a safety setting. It is the product.

    Every other AI tool sells you on how little you have to do. That holds right up to the moment the thing being automated is a message to the client who is deciding whether to pay you.

    Cognivex does an hour of work unprompted and then stops. The step where something leaves your business stays yours — not as a switch you could turn off, but as the shape of the thing.

    • No autonomous mode waiting behind a settings page.
    • No category approvals — you approve that item, not that kind of item.
    • No silent sends, including when it is confident.

    You are not supervising a machine that would rather you were not there. You are signing work that is already done.

    You are the operations department.

    You answer the email, send the invoice and chase the payment. There is nobody to hand that to, because you are also the person doing the work the business is paid for.

    Not a team with an operations hire. Not someone who wants to learn to prompt an AI properly first.
